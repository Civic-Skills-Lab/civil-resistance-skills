# Legal, Permit, and Rights Check

Use this reference to identify legal questions and documentation needs. This is not legal advice; laws and enforcement practices are jurisdiction-specific.

## Legal Posture Questions

- What jurisdiction, venue, street, sidewalk, park, campus, federal land, or private property is involved?
- Does the march stay on sidewalks, enter streets, require street closure, use amplified sound, or obstruct vehicle/pedestrian traffic?
- Are permits, notice, insurance, sound permissions, or park/event approvals required or customary?
- Is the event a planned march, spontaneous response, rally with short procession, or hybrid action?
- Are there recent precedents for similar marches on this route?
- Are counterprotesters likely, and what rules protect or constrain all sides?
- Are participants likely to record in public spaces, and what local rules or risks affect recording?

## Information to Verify

- official permit or notice process and deadlines
- public forum status and site rules
- time, place, and manner restrictions
- sound amplification rules
- traffic, sidewalk, and crosswalk rules
- dispersal order standards and recent enforcement patterns
- legal observer availability and role boundaries
- legal support contact or referral path

## Legal Observer Coordination

Legal observers are distinct from marshals, spokespeople, and crowd managers. If legal observers are involved, the plan should state:

- observer role and point of contact
- how observers recognize legal, medical, and communications leads
- what information observers should record
- where observers should position themselves without directing the crowd
- how incident notes are preserved after the event

## Boundaries

Do not claim a permit exists unless the user provides it or a public source confirms it. Do not advise ignoring, evading, or concealing violations of law, venue rules, or lawful safety directions.
