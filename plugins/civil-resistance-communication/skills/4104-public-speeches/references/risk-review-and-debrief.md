# Risk Review and Debrief

Use this reference before delivery and after the speech. It is a preparation and learning checklist, not legal advice.

## Pre-Delivery Risk Review

Check the draft or outline for:

- **Attribution:** The speaker does not claim to represent people, organizations, or communities without authority or recognition.
- **Evidence:** Factual claims are sourced or clearly framed as user experience, opinion, or allegation.
- **Defamation risk:** The speech avoids unsupported accusations about named people or organizations.
- **Incitement risk:** The speech does not encourage violence, threats, harassment, property damage, or unlawful interference.
- **Coercion risk:** The call to action does not pressure vulnerable people or imply retaliation.
- **Deception risk:** The speech does not hide sponsorship, fabricate support, fake identity, or misstate the event's purpose.
- **Venue / platform rules:** The plan respects known time, place, manner, recording, amplification, and access constraints.
- **Privacy:** Personal stories have consent or are anonymized enough to avoid exposure.
- **Counter-frame:** The speech does not repeat an opponent's frame so strongly that it spreads it.

## Rehearsal Feedback Checklist

During rehearsal, listen for:

- The speaker can state the core claim without reading.
- The audience can identify the ask after one pass.
- The opening creates relevance quickly.
- The evidence supports the claim without slowing the speech.
- The voice, pace, pause, and body language match the venue and tone.
- The speaker can cut to a shorter version without losing the point.
- The strongest line is accurate enough for press or social reuse.

Feedback should address the speech, not the speaker's worth. Note what worked, what confused listeners, what felt unsupported, and what should be cut.

## Final-Draft Review

Before releasing an Application Mode final package, check:

- the full draft matches the chosen outline and stated length
- the short version preserves the core claim, one proof point, and the endpoint
- the quote-safe line is accurate without surrounding context
- transitions work when read aloud
- every factual claim is sourced, labeled as user experience, or softened as opinion
- flagged risks are either resolved, qualified, or surfaced to the user

## Documentation and Media Notes

- Decide who records full video, short clips, stills, and quote notes.
- Capture the speaker's name, role, event name, date, and location accurately.
- Prepare the media line and source links before the event if press follow-up is likely.
- Preserve context around any clip likely to circulate.
- Avoid publishing sensitive personal details or faces where consent or safety is unclear.

## Post-Speech Debrief

Capture:

- turnout or audience estimate
- whether the intended audience heard or saw the speech
- strongest repeated line
- listener questions or objections
- press, social, or institutional pickup
- distortions, hostile frames, or factual challenges
- legal, safety, venue, or documentation incidents
- whether the endpoint produced action
- what to keep, cut, research, or rehearse before reuse

## Red Flags Before Reuse

Revise before reusing the speech if facts changed, a claim was challenged, the audience misunderstood the ask, opponents successfully reframed the message, the speaker's authority changed, or the venue/channel is meaningfully different.
