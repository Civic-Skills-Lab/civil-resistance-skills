# Public Speech Research Protocol

Use this reference when the topic is concrete enough to research and tools or user-provided materials are available. Research supports accuracy and audience fit; it does not replace the user's judgment, lived context, or legal advice.

## Research Order

1. Start with user-provided facts, documents, names, dates, and constraints.
2. Identify what the speech must not get wrong: named institutions, dates, decisions, legal posture, numbers, quotes, and attributions.
3. Use public sources first: official records, institutional pages, reputable news, public statements, public datasets, campaign or organization pages, and credible civil-society reports.
4. Note source reliability and recency. Prefer primary records for dates, decisions, names, legal status, and numerical claims.
5. Record source links for factual claims. Do not invent citations, private facts, confidential motives, or quotes.
6. If sources conflict, mark the contradiction, identify the strongest source for each side, and avoid using the disputed claim as a central proof point unless the speech explicitly names the uncertainty.

## Label Every Working Note

Use three labels in the research notes:

- **Verified fact:** Publicly checkable and sourced.
- **User claim:** Supplied by the user but not independently checked.
- **Assumption:** Plausible inference that still needs confirmation.

If the user wants to include an allegation about a person or organization, ask for public evidence and suggest neutral wording until the claim is verified.

## Audience and Frame Scan

Research should answer:

- What does the primary audience likely already know, believe, fear, or misunderstand?
- Which values or practical concerns does this audience already recognize?
- What is the dominant public frame around the issue?
- What counter-frame might opponents use?
- What short factual correction or reframing can keep the speech accurate without amplifying the opponent's frame?
- Which examples humanize the issue without exposing private personal details?

## Stakeholder and Institution Map

Identify:

- decision-makers or institutions named in the issue
- affected constituency and who can credibly speak from it
- allies or validators whose public statements can be cited
- press or public observers likely to repeat one line
- legal, venue, or organizational constraints that should shape wording

## Boundaries

Do not seek private personal data, dox targets, fabricate evidence, launder speculation into fact, or advise the user to evade law, venue rules, platform rules, or safety constraints.
