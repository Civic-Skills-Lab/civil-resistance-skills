# Communications, Documentation, and Debrief

Use this reference to make the march legible to participants, observers, press, and later audiences.

## Message Frame

- **Purpose line:** why people are marching.
- **Audience:** who should notice or respond.
- **Demand / moral claim:** the sentence that should survive coverage.
- **Visual identity:** banner, signs, colors, symbols, or route imagery.
- **Endpoint action:** petition, sign-up, public comment, meeting request, donation, volunteer shift, or next event.
- **Counter-frame:** likely criticism or misunderstanding and the accurate response frame.

## Press and Public Materials

Prepare:

- route map or route description
- start/end time and location
- organizer or convenor background
- speaker or endpoint information
- quote-safe line
- public facts and source links
- media contact
- accessibility and safety notes safe to publish

## Documentation Plan

Assign coverage for:

- opening ranks and banner
- wide shots showing turnout density
- route landmarks or symbolic sites
- endpoint crowd and speakers
- accessibility supports and participant care, when consent and safety allow
- incidents, if they occur, with context and timestamps

## Turnout Estimate Method

Record:

- estimate method: manual count, segment count, photo/video estimate, partner counts, police/press estimates, or range
- time of estimate
- vantage point or source
- uncertainty range
- independent corroboration if available

## Post-Event Debrief

Capture:

- turnout estimate and method
- whether intended audience saw or responded
- press/social pickup
- strongest repeated line or image
- route/logistics issues
- accessibility gaps
- legal, medical, safety, or documentation incidents
- opponent or counter-frame distortions
- whether endpoint action happened
- what to keep, change, verify, or cut before the next march
