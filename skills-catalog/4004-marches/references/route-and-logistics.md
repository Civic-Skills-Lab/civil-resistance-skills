# Route and Logistics Planning

Use this reference to test whether the march route and practical setup fit the purpose, turnout, legal posture, and participant needs.

## Route Fit

- **Start point:** visible, accessible, easy to find, and not oversized for expected turnout.
- **Object of attention:** institution, symbolic site, neighborhood, workplace, commercial district, or public space the route makes legible.
- **Endpoint:** practical closing location with room for exit, speech, vigil, sign-up, petition, or structured dispersal.
- **Route story:** what the route itself communicates, not only where it moves.
- **Turnout fit:** route width and start/end scale should match likely turnout range.
- **Sensitive frontage:** schools, hospitals, small businesses, religious sites, residences, or communities that may be affected.

## Scout the Route

Prefer public information and user-provided local knowledge. If scouting is possible, record:

- chokepoints: bridges, tunnels, narrow sidewalks, construction, fences, transit pinch points
- exits and fallback meeting points
- accessible paths, curb cuts, ramps, seating/rest points, bathrooms, and water access
- transit arrival/departure points and parking constraints
- weather exposure: heat, cold, rain, shade, wind, lighting
- sound constraints and where speakers or megaphones can be heard
- documentation vantage points for start, column density, endpoint, and wide shots

## Logistics Checklist

- route map or plain-language route description
- start/end times and expected duration
- setup and teardown needs
- signs, banners, sound, batteries, chargers, lights, trash plan
- water, first aid, rest areas, toilets, and accessibility supports
- communications channel for role leads
- participant arrival and exit instructions

## Guardrails

Do not advise blocking traffic, occupying restricted property, bypassing access controls, hiding hazardous materials, or evading lawful safety constraints. If the plan depends on street closure, sound amplification, or large-crowd controls, flag the need for jurisdiction-specific legal or permit review.
